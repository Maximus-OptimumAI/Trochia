================================
ONE-LINE PITCH
================================

Vanta builds an autonomous warehouse-floor robot that does the one job no human warehouse worker likes doing — the overnight cycle count of pallet positions in high-bay racking — so distribution-center operators can know their inventory is right at 6am every morning without paying for a night shift.

================================
WHAT WE'VE BUILT (PRODUCT)
================================

A 1.4m-tall autonomous mobile robot ("V1") that drives the aisles of a high-bay warehouse overnight, climbs an integrated telescoping mast to reach pallet positions up to 11m, and uses a fusion of LiDAR + RFID + camera-based label OCR to confirm what pallet is in each rack position. The robot reports back to the warehouse management system (WMS) by morning with a full cycle-count audit and a list of mis-positioned pallets.

The painful problem we solve: every large DC runs a "cycle count" — a continuous audit of where every pallet actually is, vs. where the WMS thinks it is. Today this is done by humans on forklifts during the day shift, which is slow, expensive, and bumps the cycle count to about once every 90 days per pallet position. With Vanta running overnight, every pallet position in a 30,000-position DC gets counted every 4 nights. The inventory accuracy improvement is typically 4–6% — which on a $40M inventory holding is $1.6–2.4M of "found" inventory per year.

V1 has done 47 nights of running in customer DCs as of this past Sunday. We've had one major failure (a battery thermal event that triggered our safety abort and shut down the robot mid-aisle — no damage, no injury, no DC downtime, but a humbling Monday morning). We've also had ~14 minor faults — mostly OCR misreads on damaged shipping labels, which is a known limitation we're solving in V1.2.
